# Contact briefs

One brief per contact: the pitch on the back of the box, in four parts. Written
for the #368 audit so that every letter we author can be checked against what the
contact is *for*. The design lives in SPEC §8 and `docs/mechanics/`; where a brief
and those disagree, they win and the brief is wrong.

1. **Who he is** — what he does, and why you write to him.
2. **What he wants** — and how you keep him loyal.
3. **What loyalty buys you.**
4. **When he turns.**

The player hears *duke*, never *rival* (CLAUDE.md, Terminology).

**Order:** the Crown's officers, the colony's own, then the court and the neighbours.

---

# The Crown's officers

---

## Steward

*The Crown's purse, and he would like it fuller.*

**Who he is.** The Crown officer who keeps the colony's books (SPEC §8.1). You
set the duty by writing to him: the base rate, and a rate for each resource. He
reports what the colony earned, warns when the books are hurting your standing,
and tells you when a town stops trading in protest. He is the one officer you ask
to do what he already wants to do (`the-steward.md` §1).

**What he wants.** Higher duty and more revenue. Every month he is told to lower
a rate costs him a little more patience, and he lets you know. Keep him loyal by
meeting the revenue targets you accept, and by not starving the Crown's
income for the colony's comfort.

**What loyalty buys you.**
- **Your rates, as you set them.** While standing holds he never refuses a tax
  order (§2). Loyalty decides whether a deep cut lands at once or in stages.
- **Warning in time**: concern, then alarm, before the Chancellor writes.
- **Tax forgiveness**: he may pay part of one resource's duty himself, for ever
  (`crown-demands.md` §10).
- He thanks the colony for goods it sends unasked (#472).

**When he turns.**
- Your cuts arrive late or by halves.
- **When standing is lost and he thinks little of you, he raises the duty over
  your head** on the colony's busiest trade, and tells you afterwards
  (SPEC §10.2).

**Design:** SPEC §8.1, §10.2; `the-steward.md`, `crown-demands.md`.

---

## Chancellor

*The Crown's political voice. He has been expecting you to fail.*

**Who he is.** The officer who speaks for the court (SPEC §8.1). He announces the
Squeeze, the Crown's ever-growing demands, from year two. He sends the formal
warnings as your standing falls, the news of defeats abroad, and in the end the
letter that says the Crown will pay no more of your promises
(`crown-standing.md` §3). Your standing with the Crown is the run, and he is
the man who tells you where it stands.

**What he wants.** To be proved right. He starts out thinking very little of
you, and enjoys the bad news (SPEC §8.1). What earns his regard is discipline:
answer a warning by promising to **retrench**, then keep the Crown's payouts
within the colony's revenue for six months.

**What loyalty buys you.**
- Very little directly. His warnings come either way, and the stakes are your
  **standing**, not his mood.
- The *well connected at court* quirk needs him to think well of you.
- ⚠ No benefit of his loyalty is designed yet. He is deferred, not exempt.

**When he turns.**
- A broken promise to retrench he remembers, and the warnings grow colder.
- Behind him stands the Crown's refusal: a final warning, a short window, then
  **no more payments on your word**, and from there the last chance.
- ⚠ No penalty of his own disloyalty is designed yet.

**Design:** SPEC §8.1, §10.3; `crown-standing.md`, `crown-demands.md`,
`endings.md`.

---

## Marshal

*He runs the Crown's wars, and your colony is a supply depot.*

**Who he is.** The Crown officer fighting the Crown's wars overseas
(SPEC §8.1). He demands gold and goods (iron, guns, food, horses) for a war you
will never see and cannot influence (`the-marshal.md` §8). He is also the only
man who can send you **Crown troops**: a garrison, a patrol, or an army to put
down a rebellion, which are the only thing that will fight rebels.

**What he wants.** Supplies, sent on time and in full. Helping you is pure cost
to him, so his first answer is no (§1). Keep him loyal by meeting his demands,
paying for his troops in full, keeping your word, and not wasting his men. He
takes casualties hard, and a troops bill left unpaid harder than any other
officer.

**What loyalty buys you.**
- **Troops**, which the Crown feeds. He never refuses troops you pay for in
  full.
- A garrison for a town that comes back from rebellion.
- His regard is what lets you ask at all: below a point, the troops letter is
  not on your desk.

**When he turns.**
- He sends **less than you asked**, or nothing.
- After a warning he **calls the troops home**.
- He plays every threat down in his reports.
- In the end it is his refusal to send more men that opens the road to
  **independence**.

**Design:** SPEC §8.1, §12.6; `the-marshal.md`, `crown-demands.md`.

---

## Provost

*The officer through whom you buy growth.*

**Who he is.** The Crown officer for the colony's people (SPEC §8.1,
`the-provost.md` §1). He decides who sails: how many settlers, how well
provisioned, how many experts, what livestock. He also proposes towns the Crown
will found for you, and keeps a modest curriculum for the colony's schooling. He
writes as soon as there is a town to send people to.

**What he wants.** Two things: his bills paid, and a learned colony. His regard
rises when the towns are educated whether or not you paid him (§5), and the
curriculum is the cheapest way to keep him content. His advice is always the
same: spend more.

**What loyalty buys you.**
- **People.** Settlers and experts, even to towns with no school of their own.
- **Crown foundings**, which arrive by sea with a character already set.
- **Education** in every town through his curriculum.
- When all is lost, the **town begun again by sea**.

**When he turns.**
- He **lets lapse** whatever he will not pay for himself.
- He refuses the changes he dislikes.
- **He goes round you**: he presses a town toward schooling himself, and its
  governor writes that it was not your letter that moved him (#401).

**Design:** SPEC §8.1; `the-provost.md`, `immigration.md`, `founding-towns.md`.

## Diplomat

*The Crown's Resident: the only man in the colony with no stake in it.*

**Who he is.** A Crown officer who lives in one of your towns (SPEC §8.1,
`the-diplomat.md` §1). He watches every town (its mood, its safety, its hunger,
its comforts) and tells you plainly which is worst and what he would do about
it. On the town he lives in he tells you everything: each man's intent, his
loyalty, every order refused. He is your one disinterested pair of eyes.

**What he wants.** A colony that lives decently and eats. He takes a dark view
of both. For himself he wants a little money now and then, and to be moved when
his town turns dangerous. Grant those and he stays yours.

**What loyalty buys you.**
- **The truth, in full**, with his suggestion attached.
- **He wins a governor back.** Moved to a town that is slipping, he cultivates
  its governor at your expense, and that man's loyalty climbs for as long as
  you pay. It is the only lever you have on a governor who will not listen to
  you.

**When he turns.**
- His reports cool, then the detail of his own town stops, then he writes only
  to ask for money or a move. Granting either brings him back.
- Leave him in a town that rebels and he dies there, and there is no
  replacement.

**Design:** SPEC §8.1; `the-diplomat.md`.

# The colony

---

## Governor

*One man, one town, and everything that happens in it.*

**Who he is.** Every town has one, elected by the settlers who founded it
(SPEC §8.2, §11.4). He runs the town; you never do. He chooses its projects,
works its fields, deals with the tribe over the hill and decides what it is
*for*. You can argue for a course (profit, growth, defence, new settlements)
and he weighs your letter against his own judgement. He is your eyes on the
ground and your only hands in the colony: every shipment, every embargo, every
answer to a tribe goes through him.

**What he wants.** A town that does well by its people: full stores, fair
duty, comforts, safety, and to be left to judge his own ground. Keep him loyal
by answering when he asks, sending what you promised, paying fairly for what you
take, and arguing with him rather than commanding him. He remembers every
kindness, and every refusal longer. A town in trouble can be bought back with
gold to its purse.

**What loyalty buys you.**
- He **does what you urge**, and ships what you ask.
- He **asks before he acts**: how to answer a tribe's grievance, or its plea for
  help.
- He **warns you**: a duke's men next door, his town's mood, the course he has
  settled on.
- **His good word**: put his standing behind the Crown and every other man in
  his town thinks better of you (#474).

**When he turns.**
- He **decides alone and tells you afterwards**, if at all.
- He **refuses your orders and ships nothing**.
- At the bottom he **prepares for rebellion**: walls, powder and grain laid in,
  "nothing to report".
- His town may **declare**, stop paying, and deal with dukes and tribes on its
  own. A sour governor founds sour daughter towns.
- The Diplomat may yet buy him back, if he lives there.

**Sheet:** `correspondence-audit.md` §1. **Design:** SPEC §8.2, §11.3–11.4,
§12.3; `governor-objectives.md`, `governor-agendas.md`, `founding-towns.md`.

## Clergyman

*The voice of the poorest people in town.*

**Who he is.** He comes with the church (`institutional-contacts.md` §3). He
lives in his town and speaks for those at the bottom of it, and the town
listens: when he thinks well of the Crown, so do they. His church's sense of
safety rises and falls with him.

**What he wants.** A town where the poorest are not miserable: health and a
living, not pleasures. He disapproves of the ale house and of guns. He asks for
charity for the poor, a festival, a holy day free of duty, and once, a colony of
pilgrims.

**What loyalty buys you.**
- **A quiet town.** He holds its rebel sentiment down, hard.
- His church's safety, in full.
- **Duty waived for a festival or a holy day**: the town happier, the Crown's
  income lighter.
- **Pilgrims**: a third way to found a town, people bought with gold.

**When he turns.**
- **He preaches against you**, and his town's sentiment climbs fast.
- A church the town has stopped believing in.
- ⚠ A betrayal of his own is not yet designed (§6).

**Design:** `institutional-contacts.md`.

## Journalist

*The only contact whose cooperation is the danger.*

**Who he is.** He comes with the printing press (`institutional-contacts.md`
§3), and he weighs with his town as much as its governor does. He writes about
the **worst** town in the colony, not the average, and the more towns you plant
the harder he is to please.

**What he wants.** A programme: lower duties, the Crown's troops sent home,
immigration paid for properly. Not money. Granting it pleases him, and every
item of it makes a rebellion easier to win (§3). Keeping him loyal is building
the thing that leaves.

**What loyalty buys you.**
- **His town at peace**, held down at a governor's weight.
- **Public Relations**, a policy: every governor in the colony thinks a little
  better of the Crown each month.
- **Crown Sentiment**, a policy: the colony feels its duties as a quarter
  lighter than they are.

**When he turns.**
- His town's sentiment climbs at a governor's weight.
- ⚠ Turning on you in print is not yet designed (§6).

**Design:** `institutional-contacts.md`.

## Quartermaster

*A contract that unmakes the man who grants it.*

**Who he is.** He comes with the gunsmith (`institutional-contacts.md` §3). He
counts the colony's guns, in its stores and in its companies' hands, and finds
there are never enough.

**What he wants.** A colony that is armed. Iron, gold, help moving guns, and the
duty on iron lowered. He always says stocks are low and the duty too high, and
the player can check. When the gunsmith's machines break, he asks for the gold
to mend them, or the guns stop.

**What loyalty buys you.**
- **The gun contract**, a policy: the Crown pays more for guns, towns turn to
  making them, and it is very lucrative. The other residents complain, and the
  contract slowly sours him too, so it closes itself.

**When he turns.**
- No contract.
- ⚠ A betrayal of his own is not yet designed (§6). He is a placeholder in the
  doc.

**Design:** `institutional-contacts.md`.

## Scholar

*Peace, luxuries, and learning for its own sake.*

**Who he is.** He comes with the library (`institutional-contacts.md` §3). The
library teaches as well as he does. He is the only man who can **move experts
between towns**: the Provost supplies them, and the scholar distributes them.

**What he wants.** An educated colony **at peace**. He blames you for any
battle, even a duke's attack. He asks for gold, an expert, lower duty on
luxuries, and for you to press the Provost on the curriculum.

**What loyalty buys you.**
- A library that teaches well.
- **Travelling experts**, a policy: a little money for travel, and he moves
  experts to the towns that need them.

**When he turns.**
- A slighted scholar teaches badly.
- His town's sentiment rises a little.
- Left to move experts on his own, he keeps them in his own library.

**Design:** `institutional-contacts.md`.

# The court and the neighbours

---

## Patron

*A powerful man at home with an interest in your colony, for a while.*

**Who he is.** A merchant, noble, bishop or scholar the Crown sends your way
(SPEC §8.3). He has a **specialty** (a crop, a herd, experts, troops, gold, or
a grudge against a duke) and a **need** of his own. He writes first and you
answer; you never write to him unasked. He stays two to four years, gives six
months' notice, and goes. Until his specialty comes into play he is a leech you
can make use of (`patrons.md` §1).

**What he wants.** His need shipped to him, gold, and a colony he can be seen
to back. Some care about more: the pragmatic one watches your books, the
respectable one your rebels, the doctrinaire one what you sell. Keep him loyal by
granting what he asks when you can, and by being a name worth knowing at court.

**What loyalty buys you.**
- **Prestige**, as each deed is done and again when he leaves on good terms.
- **His specialty**, offered once he thinks well enough of you:
  - a better price or yield for a resource or a herd;
  - an expert for a town with a library;
  - a company the Crown feeds;
  - gold to the Crown;
  - a year of trouble for one duke, which lets you send that duke's next
    tribute demand to the patron's house instead of paying it.
- **A policy of his still standing when he leaves becomes yours, for ever, and
  free.**

**When he turns.**
- **No offers.** At a ruinous name, none at all.
- The thin-skinned feel a refusal more. The loudmouth costs you prestige
  outright. The well-connected **talk at court**, and the Crown's own officers
  think less of you for it.
- The respectable **walk out** when a town declares, taking his policies with
  him.
- A sour farewell **banks against your prestige for good**.

**Design:** SPEC §8.3; `patrons.md`.

## Duke

*He wants your land, and he can wait.*

**Who he is.** Three neighbouring empires, one duke each, and he is all you will
ever hear of his (SPEC §8.4, §12.4). He arrives when the Squeeze turns his eye
on you, and from then on he writes for one reason: **tribute**. You answer; you
never write first.

**What he wants.** You **paying, and weak**. He thinks less of you as the colony
grows in towns, people and land, and when you refuse him. Paying buys his regard
and nothing else: **it never makes a friend of him** (SPEC §8.4). Every payment
costs gold, standing and prestige, and the court does not forget it.

**What loyalty buys you.** *Time.*
- **High:** reasonable demands, and he forgives a missed payment.
- **Middling:** dearer demands, less patience.
- At either, **he does not attack.**
- No trade and no help, ever. A patron with a grudge can send one demand
  elsewhere for you.

**When he turns.**
- **Low:** he burns improvements, parks soldiers and ships on your fields so
  they cannot be worked, and strikes expeditions on the road.
- **At the bottom, open war on your towns**, and that is a latch: no payment and
  no patron brings him back.
- Once in his life he **backs a rebel town** with gold, stores and a company
  (`rival-pressure.md` §8).

**Design:** SPEC §8.4, §12.4; `rival-pressure.md`.

## Commander

*The man at the head of the column, an ocean from your desk.*

**Who he is.** An officer leading a company: Crown troops the Marshal lends you,
or a large company the colony raises (SPEC §8.6; `commanders.md` §1–2). He
reports from the field and asks what his men are for. **You never command a
battle** (SPEC §12.6): you argue for attack, hold, march or withdraw, and he
weighs your letter against the ground in front of him.

**What he wants.** To keep his men alive and fed, to win, and to have a command
at all; a veteran left without one argues for raising another. Keep him loyal by
supplying him and listening when he reports. ⚠ Whether spending his men costs
his regard is open (`commanders.md` §10).

**What loyalty buys you.**
- He **follows your urging**: attack, hold, withdraw, spare or punish a rebel
  town.
- He **asks before he makes an example** of a rebel town.
- Experience is his own, earned in battle: bearing, marches, siegecraft.

**When he turns.**
- He **fights his own war**: your urging scores below his own judgement, and
  Crown troops refuse orders against Crown interests (SPEC §12.6).
- He **makes an example of a rebel town and tells you afterwards**.
- ⚠ What low loyalty does beyond that is not yet designed (`commanders.md`
  §10).

**Design:** SPEC §8.6, §12.6; `commanders.md`, `battles.md`.
